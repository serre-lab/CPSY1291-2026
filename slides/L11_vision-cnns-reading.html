Slide 10

Regularization 2: dropout

  • Dropout: at each training step, switch off a random half of the hidden units; all are on at test time
  • Often with the weight penalty, sometimes L1 (absolute values): each cuts the freedom to fit noise
Srivastava et al. JMLR 2014

No more data? Make altered copies of it

Augmentation in PyTorch: a transform pipeline

transforms.Compose([ transforms.RandomResizedCrop(224, scale=(0.5, 1.0)), # Crop and resize transforms.RandomHorizontalFlip(), # Flip horizontally at random transforms.RandomRotation(15), # Rotate by up to 15 degrees transforms.ColorJitter(0.3, 0.3, 0.3, 0.05), # Vary colour and brightness transforms.ToTensor(), # Convert to channel-first floats in [0, 1] transforms.Normalize(IMAGENET_MEAN, IMAGENET_STD), # Standardize channels ])
  • Each epoch draws a fresh random version of every image, so the network rarely sees the same picture twice. Flips that would change the label stay out: a 6 is not a 9

Slide 19

Dense versus convolutional: where the weights go

  • The perceptron wires every pixel to every unit. The convolutional layer wires each unit to a patch, and every unit shares the same weights: a feature detector copied across the image

Return to contents

Slide 20

Count the weights: a dense layer on an image

  • Flatten a 224 × 224 colour image: one dense unit needs 224 · 224 · 3 = 150,528 weights, and it ignores which pixels are neighbours
  • AlexNet's first layer instead learns 96 small filters of 11 × 11 × 3 and reuses each one at every position: 34,848 weights, about 400 times fewer than 96 dense units
  • An object can sit anywhere in the image, so the detector is needed at every position: local filters, the same everywhere
Krizhevsky, Sutskever & Hinton NeurIPS 2012

The convolutional layer, in notation

𝐚[l]=g(𝐖[l]𝐚[l−1]+𝐛[l])\mathbf{a}^{[l]} = g(\mathbf{W}^{[l]}\mathbf{a}^{[l-1]} + \mathbf{b}^{[l]})

A[l]=g(K[l]∗A[l−1]+𝐛[l])\fmap{l} = \textcolor{#7A4FA3}{g}\big(\textcolor{#B5396B}{\kernel{l}} \conv \fmap{l-1} + \textcolor{#3F7A6B}{\mathbf{b}^{[l]}}\big)

Slide 30

Retina and LGN: Kuffler's centre–surround cells

  • A cell's receptive field: the patch of the visual field where light changes its firing. For a retinal ganglion or LGN cell, light on the + centre excites, on the − surround inhibits (left: on-centre, and its mirror image, off-centre); uniform light does nothing. Right, Kuffler's records: these cells report local contrast, not brightness
Kuffler J Neurophysiol 1953

Slide 48

Recap

  • Generalize better: a penalty on the weights (weight decay, ridge), dropout, more or altered data, with λ chosen on validation data
  • Brain: center–surround, simple cells wired from rows of LGN cells, complex cells that pool them; receptive fields mapped by hand or by reverse correlation
  • Network: convolution (one filter, shared), stride and pooling, global average pooling to a feature vector
